Irishima’s vein杁島

The island is not a twist. It is the mineral the present has been swimming in since page one, finally shown as work.

About 250 kilograms of Datenseki are known. One pair of eyes ever made it safe. The Seitei War is, at the mineral level, a fight over a vein that showed after an earthquake on Irishima and a nation that rose from the sea. Everything else (clans, contracts, flowers, goldfish) is what people did with a quarter-ton of rock. Part 2’s job is to make that sentence expensive. Not a caption. Hours. Heat. A princess who can see. A smith who has not yet agreed to look.

The Mikaboshi were sorcerer kings the Soga drove off the mainland more than a thousand years ago. They survived under the sea in a habitat of branches, bodies adapted to Datenseki, and came back when the vein showed. Ariu’s insects, poisoned air, and hardened flesh are the royal kit. After chapter 119, a camp of explainers said Magatsumi is those insects recast in Kunishige’s steel. The camp is useful. It is not yet a caption this essay will print as fact. What is fact: once Magatsumi entered the field, Japan could walk onto the island. What is fact: after a treaty, Akemura used Malediction anyway. About 200,000 civilians. The Kamunabi filed it as victory. The war over a vein ended as a myth about heroes. Part 2 is the invoice before the filing.

Talks are a battlefield

Chapters 117 through 121 are titled as talks, not battles. That is the book’s joke and its thesis. A princess with foresight is a strategic weapon. A bureau that wants Datenseki without admitting it needs a picky civilian smith is a strategic coward. Hasumi’s lab fails. Shiba already knows whose eyes to hire. Mashiro does not want to steal ore for a weapons dealer. Giyu might trade Chiaki. Hiroto can crush a man with gravity and still die to a prince who grew up inside the mineral. The Enchanted Blades have not been forged. The war is already happening as a meeting. Jump’s English readers who wanted another hotel fight got a conference table. The table is the point. Malediction is what you get when the conference is later declared insufficient by the man who held the strongest sword.

Kunishige, in this past, is not the hermit over a cellar. He is a man who will not sell to people he cannot stand, which means he barely eats. Subaru will like him because both of them are smiths who kept a civilian craft (sushi, pickiness) next to a lethal one. Enten’s goldfish are still years away. The household language of the seventh blade is being assembled as relationships, not as a design document. Chiaki is hope. Shiba is the friend who will tell him not to close his eyes in the fire. Akemura is not yet the uncle in the basement. He is the brother a princess loves. Part 1 taught you to hate the Sword Master. Part 2 is making you watch the country build him.

Smelting as ethics

Chapters 125 through 129 refuse montage. Smelting, Fire, Smelting, Smelting, Ironworks. Hokazono talked to a real swordsmith. The process is dangerous enough that Kunishige nearly dies in the shop in chapter 129, and the thing that pulls him back is not patriotism. It is Chiaki. The mineral wants to pop the user. The eyes are the only stabilizer. Sojo will later try to fake those eyes with Kyonagi flesh and will pop himself. Tenri will try a half-stable stone to impress Kyora and will pop himself. The present-tense plot is full of people dying of the lesson Part 2 is currently teaching in real time: Datenseki without Kunishige is a crater. Datenseki with Kunishige is a war crime with a warranty.

True Realm, in the present, is the wielder’s brief locking with steel. In the past, the brief is still being written by a government. Kill the enemy. Protect the mainland. Make the vein usable. Those are not Chihiro’s brief. Chihiro’s brief, once he learns it, is fewer blades. Enten exists because this smelting succeeded too well. If you love Part 2’s craft and skip the Malediction essay, you are reading Sojo’s book: the work without the household. If you love the flowers essay and skip the smelting, you are reading the Kamunabi’s press release: the crime without the kiln. The vein requires both essays.

Atlantis was always the clock

Chapter 113’s color page (the island English Twitter called Japan’s Atlantis) is not Part 2’s first image. It is Part 1 admitting the clock. Shokoku rose about twenty-two years before the present. The war ends eighteen years before. Enten is forged about fifteen years after the war. The raid is three years before page one. The numbers were always a mineral’s biography. Part 2 finally puts a princess and a kiln on the same timeline instead of a revenge teen and a stolen katana. The YouTube will keep arguing cour 1 endings and paternity. This essay is for the quieter claim: the series was never only about who holds the swords. It was about who was willing to turn a quarter-ton of island into a personality, and who was sorry afterward, and who was not.

When chapter 130 prints, the ironworks may finish a blade. Magatsumi is the sixth forged in some tallies and the strongest in all of them. Cloud Gouger is weather. Tobimune is support. Kumeyuri is fifth. Official chapters: VIZ / MANGA Plus. Related: Part 2 page, Datenseki, Malediction, Chiaki.
